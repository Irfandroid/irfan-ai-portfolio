# Portfolio Product Truth

## Purpose

This site is the professional portfolio of Muhamad Irfan Afriyansyah. Its job is to help recruiters, hiring managers, and technical collaborators quickly understand his direction, evidence, and working style in AI engineering.

## Audience

- Recruiters and hiring managers evaluating AI Engineering, Machine Learning, and data-focused roles.
- Engineering leads looking for clear evidence of retrieval, RAG, evaluation, and dependable ML systems thinking.
- Collaborators who want a concise view of technical judgment, communication, and learning trajectory.

## Core message

Irfan builds data-informed AI systems that are understandable, testable, and grounded in real user needs. The portfolio should communicate a trajectory from data and machine learning experiments toward production-minded AI engineering.

## Voice

- Professional, direct, and specific.
- Confident without overclaiming.
- Explain technical decisions in plain language before introducing implementation detail.
- Use evidence, constraints, outcomes, and lessons instead of generic skill lists.

## Experience principles

- Keep the Google Stitch-inspired discipline as the visual foundation: one calibrated accent, strong typographic hierarchy, structured metadata, deliberate asymmetry, and clear spatial zones. The current visual world is an Editorial AI Engineer system in Zinc and Burnt Clay rather than a dashboard or generic paper template.
- Make the first screen legible within seconds: role direction, value proposition, proof point, and one clear next action.
- Treat project pages as evidence: context, architecture, technical decisions, failure paths, and next steps.
- Keep interactions subtle and purposeful. Preserve keyboard focus, touch targets, and reduced-motion preferences.
- Avoid invented metrics, vague claims, visual noise, and decorative UI that competes with the work.

## Content constraints

- Use English for the primary portfolio narrative, with established technical terms kept in their standard industry form.
- Preserve the existing project evidence and repository links.
- The CV is a public download only; there is no visitor-facing upload or edit control. The owner updates the source file and redeploys it.

## Reference library

- `emmabostian/developer-portfolios` is an inspiration directory, not a content source. Use it to study concise role positioning, visible portfolio identity, varied presentation, and direct links to inspectable work.
- Do not copy another developer's name, biography, claims, project story, visual identity, or code. Irfan's public repositories and LinkedIn remain the source of truth.
- Keep the portfolio curated. The reference directory's large volume is useful for pattern discovery, not a reason to add a gallery of other people's work.
